# BTS "Body to Body" — Music Video Script V2: Mountains to Stage

## Concept

The video begins where the project already lives — the traditional Korean mountain landscape. A golden figure (the fan/viewer) stands alone among ancient peaks. The camera pulls forward through the mountains, through a city materializing from the mist, and crashes into a stadium the instant the lyrics demand it. From there: seven members appear one by one, the concert peaks, the members vanish, the ARMY rises into the sky as constellations, and the golden figure sings Arirang alone in a rice paddy that was hidden beneath the stage all along.

The existing Scene 1 (mountains), zoom transition (buildings), and Scene 2 (stadium) are the backbone. Everything new grows from what's already built.

---

## Characters

- **The Golden Figure**: The viewer/fan. Warm gold (#D4A843). Present from the first frame — a tiny warm dot among the cold blue mountains. Barely visible. The only warm-colored element in Scene 1's palette. Not centered until the Arirang.
- **The Seven**: BTS member silhouettes. Each has a signature trait (posture, height, movement style) so fans can identify them without faces. Cyan/neon during concert sections, warm earth tones during the Arirang reunion.

### ACT 1, PART 1 — THE ANCIENT LANDSCAPE (0:00 – 0:13)

**Music**: *"I need... I need, I need..."*

**SET**: A Korean mountain range at night, seen from within. Baekdu-style peaks layered in parallax depth — near ridges dark and sharp, far ridges fading to indigo mist. Full moon stage-right, large, cratered, spilling cold white light across everything. A star field above — dense, traditional, no light pollution. At the base, dark waves crash slow against rocks. Cranes coast between the peaks. Cherry blossoms drift from unseen trees. Dancheong-patterned borders frame the entire image like a painted screen. The palette is the Obangsaek: indigo, jade, gold, cream, ink black. No modern element anywhere. This could be 1400.

**Visual**: Existing Scene 1. Night. The Korean mountain landscape in full traditional splendor — Baekdu ranges in parallax depth, stars twinkling, full moon with crater detail, cranes gliding, cherry blossoms drifting, waves at the base. Dancheong borders frame the image. Irworobongdo symbolism. The Obangsaek palette — deep indigo, jade, gold, cream, ink black.

This is Korea's soul. Ancient. Still. Waiting.

**The golden figure** is here from frame one — but you won't see it. A tiny warm gold dot, standing on the nearest mountain ridge in the foreground. The same scale as a pine tree silhouette. Invisible unless you know to look. Surrounded by the traditional palette, its warmth reads as a slightly odd star or a lantern in the distance.

**0:07 — "I need"**: The figure shifts weight. A single movement in an otherwise gently animated landscape. The cranes fly. The blossoms drift. The golden dot sways.

**Key**: This is the existing Scene 1 with one addition — the golden figure hidden in the landscape.

### ACT 1, PART 2 — THE CITY (0:07 – 0:20)

**Music**: *"I need (What you need?)... I need, I need"*

**SET**: A corridor of 3D city buildings materializing from mountain mist — blue-grey glass towers, cold geometry, lit from below by street-level glow. The buildings form a canyon with a vanishing point that pulls the eye forward. The traditional landscape is still visible behind, stretching and fading.

**0:00–0:04 — the creep**: The camera is already moving on the first frame, but barely: for three seconds the landscape holds almost still and wide, moon, cloud band and cranes all in frame, and the only tell is the dancheong border easing outward past the edges.

**0:04.3 — through the title**: "Body to Body" swells past the camera and out of frame — the first thing we travel *through* rather than cut away from.

**0:06.3 — through the ridges**: The peaks blow past on both sides and the city is already there underneath, materialising out of the haze since 0:04.7.

**0:07.24 — "I need"**: The punch. The camera goes out through the back of the night sky — three shells of stars streaking past and gone exactly on the vocal — and the city is all that's left.

**0:13 — "I need (What you need?)"**: The pixel "I NEED" sign lights at the far end of the avenue; we fly through it at 0:14.3, just before it fades.

**0:14.96 — "I need, I need"**: The corridor walls light the same words in window-text as the camera enters the canyon, and the flight accelerates the whole way down it.

**0:18.65 — "I need the whole stadium to jump"**: A purple speck appears at the far end of the corridor. That is the arena.

**0:19.3–0:20.2 — the burst**: The speck opens out, still with the corridor walls streaking past either side, and fills the frame exactly on 0:20.2 — the beat the blast ends on, and the drop that shakes the whole picture.

**The golden figure** is no longer on the mountain — it's now a dot moving through the city, below the camera, running along the streets toward the stadium. One warm speck among cold blue buildings. Heading to the show.

**Key**: One unbroken twenty-second push, timed so every punch-through lands on a lyric. Nothing in Act 1 dissolves; everything is flown through.

### ACT 2, PART 1 — THE STADIUM (0:20.4 – 0:26)

**Music**: *"I need the whole stadium to jump / Put your phone down..."*

**SET**: A massive stadium interior seen from the upper tiers looking down. Curved arena structure, three tiers of seating packed with 800+ crowd silhouettes, each holding a glowing ARMY bomb (purple-pink light stick). The stage at the far end is a raised rectangular platform — dark, empty, unlit. Spotlights mounted on overhead rigs are powered but idle, sweeping the crowd, not the stage. Confetti cannons loaded but dormant. The energy is a full house waiting for a show that hasn't started.

**0:20.4 — "Put your phone down, let's get all the fun"**: The chain hands over on a matching full-frame stadium, so nothing cuts. Confetti starts here and not before — it belongs to being *inside* the arena, not to the window we were looking through. The stage is empty. The crowd is waiting. The energy is anticipation, not climax.

**0:20.4–0:22 — the drop**: The bass drop sets the whole arena shaking on the beat. Bombs pulse faster, beams sweep wider. Something is about to happen.

**0:22–0:26 — the dive**: The camera dives toward the stage — the crowd, LED walls and light towers blow past the edges of frame, the stage lights dissolve as we pass through them, and the shot lands dead-center on the stage at 0:26, on the verse's last line — the first frame of Part 2. The first member touches down at 0:25.5, half a second ahead of the camera, so the stage is already coming alive as it reaches frame. (Timeline: scene `2.1-zoom` with `hold: 1.6, zoom: 4`.)

**Key**: The existing Scene 2. The only new element is the empty stage and the sense of waiting.

### ACT 2, PART 2 — SEVEN (0:26 – 0:30)

**Music**: *"The vibe is high, let the building" → rap verse → cut on "I need some body to body"*

**SET**: On stage, landed from the dive. The Arirang cosmos backdrop behind a purple-lit floor. Seven spotlights wait in the rig, all dark.

**0:25.5–0:28 — seven arrivals**: The members appear one-by-one, 0.42s apart (Jin first, Jungkook last and centre stage, landing on 0:28), each fading in under their spotlight in their bias color. Each figure hits a signature pose, then grooves on it in a loop — bouncing on the beat, each to their own phase, while the rest are still landing. Seven lights, seven arrivals, and the entrance gets room to breathe rather than dumping all seven inside a second.

**0:29.9 — unison**: A breath after Jungkook lands, the seven crossfade into one routine: V-up → sky-point right → wings out → sky-point left, one hit per two beats, everyone bouncing on the same beat now.

**0:30 — the camera leaves**: The dance never resolves. Mid-routine, the camera starts backing away — the concert stays at full power, we're the ones who go.

**Key**: Individual grooves melting into unison — seven independent artists becoming one act the moment the last one lands.

### ACT 2, PART 3 — STAGE → MOUNTAINS (0:30 – 0:44)

**Music**: *"뭘 체면 따져 내려놔, 야 인마" → "Hop in, 좀 더 가까이 와 skin to skin" → lands on the chorus*

**SET**: Everything Acts 1 and 2 flew through, run backwards, in fourteen seconds — the longest single move in the video.

**0:30 — the pull-back**: The push-in from 0:20–0:26 plays in reverse, unhurried. The stage shrinks out of viewport-fill back into its little window at the end of the arena, the members still dancing as they recede; the crowd tiers, ARMY bombs and light towers scale back down around them. The beat shake fades out with the distance — the arena stops shaking as we leave it.

**0:34.5 — the arena closes**: The stadium collapses back into the speck it was seen from outside — the portal from Act 1 Part 2, shutting.

**0:35.4 — the flight home**: The city corridor streams past in reverse, the "I NEED" window-text walls flashing by the other way. Out of the corridor, back over the towers, past the N Seoul Tower, and the mountains rise into frame — cranes, blossoms, the moon — settling into the Act 1 landscape. No title card this time; the frame is the place, not the opening.

**0:43.5 — rest**: The peaks hold, still, for a beat.

**0:44.99 — "I need some body to body"**: The chorus lands and we cut to the road home — the same range now small on the horizon behind the farmhouse.

**Key**: The journey out is the journey in, reversed. Acts 1–2 were the trip to the show; this is the trip back, thirty seconds compressed into fourteen — slow enough to feel like distance rather than a rewind button. It buys Act 3 its geography — the audience has *seen* the mountains they'll spot behind the hanok.

### ACT 3, PART 1 — THE ROAD HOME (0:44 – 0:58)

**Music**: *"I need some body to body / All of your body beside me..."*

**SET**: A dirt road cutting straight through rice paddies at golden hour. Low stone walls line both sides. The road runs from the horizon to a small hanok farmhouse — tiled roof, paper-panel doors glowing warm from inside, a persimmon tree heavy with fruit in the yard, shoes paired neatly outside the front step. Through the open door: a low wooden table in the main room. On the far horizon behind the house, the mountain range from Act 1 — distant, small, watching. Stick figures are warm-toned silhouettes — gold, amber, soft brown — with posture that tells you everything about who they are.

Two stick figures on the road, walking toward the house. A tall one — the parent — carrying a small one on their back (업기, the Korean piggyback). The child's head rests on the parent's shoulder, legs dangling limp. Asleep or nearly. The parent walks steady, unhurried. One hand holds the child's leg. The other swings free.

Rice paddies on both sides catch the last light — shallow water turning the fields into gold mirrors. A few fireflies are already out, hovering low. The road is soft dirt, worn smooth by years of the same walk home.

**0:49 — "All of your body beside me"**: They reach the yard. The cat on the stone wall watches them come. Laundry on the line barely moves. The parent ducks through the doorway with the child still on their back. The warm light swallows both of them.

**0:51 — "저기 저 달에 닿게 손에 손"** (hand in hand reaching the moon): The parent settles the child down inside. Through the open door we see them take their place at the table. The moon rises over the house — the same moon from the mountains, watching over a different scene now.

**Key**: The road. The walk. The carry. A parent bringing a child home at the end of a day. This is the "before" — the world when the road only goes one direction: toward home.

### ACT 3, PART 2 — THE TABLE (0:58 – ~1:14)

**Music**: *"Somebody like you" × 4 → "Everybody like you"*

**SET**: Inside the hanok. The main room, seen through the open front door. The low wooden table at center. Warm light from an oil lamp or hearth — the paper walls glow amber. Simple, spare, beautiful. The room is the table and the people around it.

Stick figures around the table — a family. A grandparent sits at the head, hunched forward, elbows wide, the posture of someone who's sat in that spot for decades. A second elder beside them — frailer, slower, hands resting on the table edge. The parent from the road, settling in, turning from the child to the grandparent and back. The child, sitting on their knees to reach the table, small hands around a bowl. Maybe a sibling — another small figure, fidgeting, leaning sideways.

Steam rises from a pot in the center. Bowls at every place. Chopsticks laid out. The grandparent's hand pushes a dish toward the child. The parent serves the elders first — a small gesture that reads instantly.

**0:58 — "Somebody like you, ayy"**: The table is full. Every seat occupied. The stick figures are simple lines — but the grandparent's hunch, the child's reach, the parent turning between generations — it tells you everything. This is a family that eats together every night.

**1:02 — "Somebody like you, oh"**: The child leans against the parent's side. The grandparent sets down chopsticks and watches the table with the stillness of someone who knows this won't last. The fire flickers. The room is warm.

**1:06 — "Somebody like you, ayy"**: Outside through the open door: the road is empty and dark and peaceful. The fireflies drift over the paddies. The house is a warm box in the dark countryside. Everyone who matters is under this roof.

**1:10 — "Somebody like you, somebody like"**: Slow push in on the table. The figures are close enough that you can see the small gestures — a hand resting on another hand, a head tilting toward a shoulder. The warmth is so specific you can almost smell the rice.

**1:14 — "Everybody like you"**: The golden figure — the child — glows faintly brighter for one beat. They don't know it yet, but they're the one who will leave. For now, they're just a kid at a table, full and warm and home.

**Key**: The table full. The family complete. "Everybody like you" lands differently when you can see exactly what "everybody" means — it means *these specific people, in these specific seats, on this specific night.* This is what will be missed.

### ACT 4 — THE LIFE BETWEEN (~1:14 – ~1:24)

**Music**: *"Everybody like you" (tail) → "It's so tight / 너와의 사이 / 우리만의 그 style"*

Act 4 compresses a lifetime of the caregiver-child relationship into ten seconds. Nine beats: one industrial establishing shot, then a "before" block of three childhood vignettes plus a doorway time-marker, then an "after" block of three mirror vignettes, then the PAT — the moment the grown child finally registers how much the caregiver has aged.

**Storytelling rule**: Before/after pairs are split across the act rather than cut back-to-back. The viewer holds the early-life frame in memory and recognises the inversion four beats later. Recognition rather than punchline.

Only two characters in this arc — the caregiver and the child (golden figure). The caregiver dims a shade each beat from 4.3 onward, landing at the PAT's gold (~`CARE_GOLD_49`) by 4.9. The child stays the same warm gold throughout — they're the one who isn't watching.

---

#### 4.1 — INDUSTRIALIZATION TIMELAPSE (~1:14 – ~1:15)

**Music**: *"Everybody like you" (tail)*

**SET**: Exterior of the hanok from Act 3 — same angle, same frame. The world mutates around the stubborn warm pocket of home: day/night flips on a repeating arc, power lines stitch across the sky, persimmon seasons blur, a smokestack rises where a mountain used to be, the neighbor hanok dissolves into a concrete box, a wall of dark city buildings climbs up from the horizon, the dirt road paves itself, the window glow cools from amber to LED white, the antenna becomes a satellite dish, the AC unit bolts on.

**Key**: This is the act's establishing statement — *time is passing in this world*. It establishes the authority of compressed time. The next eight beats drop the camera into specific moments inside that passing time, so the vignettes inherit the timelapse's compression without having to re-justify it.

Industrial elements (a distant smokestack on the horizon, a faint power-pole silhouette, neighbor concrete glow) remain present as quiet background in some of the yard/road vignettes that follow.

#### 4.2 — FIRST STEPS (~1:15 – ~1:16, Before — irreducible start)

**SET**: Interior hanok. Caregiver (brightest gold) seated on the floor, arms extended forward. The toddler — barely on their feet — wobbles forward across the wood floor, hands up for balance, then tumbles into the open arms.

**Key**: The relationship begins. No "after" mirror — this is the irreducible start. Every later beat is a downstream consequence of this first reach.

#### 4.3 — PERSIMMON LIFT (~1:16 – ~1:17, Before)

**SET**: Yard at golden hour. Persimmon tree heavy with fruit. Caregiver standing, hoists the toddler overhead — child reaches up, fingers brushing a low-hanging persimmon.

Stone wall in the background. First two hash marks of the height log, low on the wall.

**Key**: Lifted up so the world is reachable. Pairs with 4.7 across the gap.

#### 4.4 — SICKBED CARE (~1:17 – ~1:18, Before)

**SET**: Interior hanok at night. Child lies on a low futon under a blanket. Caregiver kneels beside, one hand resting on the child's forehead, the other holding a steaming bowl of broth. The lamp is close.

**Key**: The hand on the forehead. Pairs with 4.6 across the gap.

#### 4.5 — DOORWAY SERIES (~1:18 – ~1:20, time-marker beat)

**SET**: Held exterior shot of the hanok doorway. Three figures transit through it across the loop:
  - Tiny child runs through, bouncy and fast
  - Older child walks in, slower, head down
  - Grown child stoops to clear the frame

The doorway never moves. Only the figures change. ~1.8 seconds per pass.

**Key**: The same threshold, three ages. Time-marker beat in the middle of the act. Same doorway as 4.9's PAT — the audience has been here before, will be here again.

#### 4.6 — SICKBED REVERSED (~1:20 – ~1:21, After of 4.4)

**SET**: Mirror composition of 4.4. The caregiver (now dimmer) lies on the futon under the blanket. The grown child kneels beside, one hand on the caregiver's forehead, the other holding the bowl.

**Key**: Same camera, same room, roles inverted. Recognition lands across the gap from 4.4 — three beats earlier, four cuts apart.

---

#### 4.7 — PERSIMMON HAND-DOWN (~1:21 – ~1:22, After of 4.3)

**SET**: Same yard, same tree, same wall — but the child is grown now and the caregiver is shorter. Grown child plucks a persimmon from a high branch and hands it down to the caregiver, who reaches up to receive.

Wall measure on the stone wall now shows eight hash marks, the top one climbing well above the caregiver's head.

**Key**: The reach has inverted. Wall confirms the height history without a word.

---

#### 4.8 — PIGGYBACK INVERSION (~1:22 – ~1:23, After of Act 3 piggyback)

**SET**: Dirt road at dusk — the same road from Act 3.2. Grown child walks beside the caregiver, carrying the bundle/water-jug — the load the caregiver used to carry. Child a half-step ahead. Caregiver walks unburdened, slower, dimmer.

**Key**: The "before" image is Act 3.2 (caregiver carrying sleeping child on their back). The audience holds that frame for the longest span — nearly the whole song — and recognises this reversal here. Longest arc, biggest payoff before the PAT lands.

---

#### 4.9 — THE PAT (~1:23 – ~1:24, climax)

**Music**: *"우리만의 그 style"*

**SET**: Interior of the hanok. The child is grown now — taller than the caregiver. A cloth-wrapped food bundle sits on the low table. Door open behind them to the road.

The caregiver reaches up — *has to reach up now* — and pats the child on the head. Same gesture they used when the child was small, inverted by scale. In this moment, the child *sees* the caregiver's gold for the first time in years. Dimmer than they remembered. The caregiver has been fading through the whole act; the child just wasn't looking.

The child hesitates — wants to stay. The caregiver pats again, firmer. *Go.*

**Key**: The pat is permission, not farewell. The moment the child registers that the caregiver is mortal. Everything Act 5 does flows from this beat.

### ACT 5 — SEPARATION (~1:24 – ~1:51)

**Music**: *"we livin' the life..." → "Sunrise, but we don't go home"*

Four beats, one composition each. The core device: **5.3 and 5.4 are the same shot in two different worlds.** The cut between them is the separation itself — two lonelinesses held side by side without comment.

The caregiver's gold continues to dim from its 4.9 PAT shade through 5.1 and 5.4. The child stays the same gold throughout. The dimming is the event.

---

#### 5.1 — THE WAVE (~1:24 – ~1:28)

**Music**: *"we livin' the life / 두 눈을 감지 않을 이 밤"*

**SET**: The road outside the hanok, dawn. The same road from Act 3.

The child walks a few paces down the road, bundle in hand. Turns. Waves. The caregiver is already in the doorway, waves back. Both hands up — matched gesture across a growing distance.

The child turns and keeps walking. The caregiver's hand stays up one beat longer than the child's, then lowers. The doorway stays open.

**Key**: Mutual wave. Not a leaving that refuses to look back — a leaving that's been *released*. Makes Act 7's return honest instead of redemptive.

---

#### 5.2 — THE STREETS (~1:28 – ~1:36)

**Music**: *"솟구치는 겨레의 마음 / Be about it, be about it / You could see about it, or you read about it"*

**SET**: A city canyon. Cold glass towers rising on both sides — the same geometry as Act 1 Part 2, but now from street level, on foot.

The child pauses at the city's edge. Looks up. Plunges in.

Grey stick figures stream past in every direction — thousands of them. Heads down, phones, bags, tired postures. All grey. The child is the only warm dot in the tide. They walk, get jostled, keep walking.

Seasonal flickers compressed fast — rain, snow, sun, rain. Each cycle, the same sidewalks, the same crowd, the same solitude at volume. The child's gold holds. It doesn't dim, but it doesn't grow either. *Preserved, not lived.*

**Key**: Loneliness has a crowd texture. Being surrounded by thousands who don't see you is being alone louder.

---

#### 5.3 — THE APARTMENT (~1:36 – ~1:43)

**Music**: *"I need some body to body / All of your body beside me"*

**SET**: A small city room. One window. A narrow counter. A bare bulb. Night into dawn.

The child at the counter with one bowl. Eating. First cuts: eating from the cloth bundle — the food the caregiver wrapped at the PAT in 4.9. Slow, careful bites. Mid-beat, a later cycle: the cloth lies empty on the counter, folded. Last cut, later still: city food in a plastic takeout container. Same pose, different bowl.

Dawn light begins to come through the window behind the child.

The chorus plays — *"I need some body to body / All of your body beside me"* — over a figure eating alone in a room made for one. The lyric is the absence. This is the first moment the video pays back the cost of the leaving.

**Key**: The bundle runs out. When the cloth goes empty, home is really gone. Everything after is borrowed.

---

#### 5.4 — THE EMPTY TABLE (~1:43 – ~1:51)

**Music**: *"저기 저 달에 닿게 손에 손, 너와 나 / Sunrise, but we don't go home"*

**SET**: Interior of the hanok. The low wooden table. Door open behind the caregiver. Road visible through the door, stretching empty to the horizon.

**Mirror 5.3's last frame.** Same camera angle. Same composition. A figure alone at a surface with a bowl. But the figure is the caregiver, the surface is the low wooden table, the "window" is the open doorway, and the view beyond is the road with nobody on it.

Two places set. One bowl steaming, one cold. The caregiver eats from the steaming one, slowly. The cold bowl is for the child. It's always been set. It will be set tomorrow.

The caregiver's gold is another shade dimmer than at the PAT (4.9) — visible decline across the act.

Dawn comes through the open door — the same dawn as 5.3's window. The lyric *"Sunrise, but we don't go home"* bridges the cut from 5.3 to 5.4. Two figures, two windows, two bowls, one sun.

**Key**: The separation made visible. The audience understands without words that this is happening *right now*, in both places, at the same dawn, and has been for a long time. The door stays open. The bowl stays set. The caregiver keeps dimming. Not the leaving — the *accepting*.

### ACT 7 — SOMEBODY LIKE YOU (1:51 – 2:22)

**Music**: *"Somebody like you" ×4 → "Everybody like you"*

**SET**: The road home, walked the other way. The child of Act 3 is an adult
now, coming back from the city. Cut in two on the lyric that turns from
*somebody* to *everybody*:

| Scene | Song | Local | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7.1 — The Road Back: Dusk** | 1:51 – 2:03 | 0–12s | Sunset. Him and two or three others. |
| **7.2 — The Road Back: Night** | 2:03 – 2:22 | 0–19s | Night. The road fills. The city lets go. |

**THE VALLEY**: There are no paddies any more — the fields were graded and
paved while he was gone. Hardpan, broken slabs, an asphalt road with lane
markings, transmission pylons marching down the verge. On the horizon, the
mountains; standing behind and well above them, the city — the end state of
4.1's industrialisation timelapse, throwing a sodium haze onto the underside
of the sky. He is walking home toward a valley that industry took.

**THE FIGURES**: He is a stick figure — the only one. Everybody else is a
capsule body and a sphere head in faded gold: present, ordinary, and never
glowing. He is the only light in either scene. (The people he knows —
the parent in the doorway, eventually the Seven — are stick figures too. The
rest of the world is pegs.)

**1:51 — "Somebody like you, ayy"**: Three-quarter rear, long lens, the city
stacked onto the ridge behind him. He walks. Two others are already with him.

**1:55 — "Somebody like you, oh"**: A fourth falls in, then a fifth. Not
clustered — each arriving at its own pace, off its own line, the way people do
who found the same road from different directions.

**1:59 — "Somebody like you, ayy"**: The camera arcs around behind them. The
house is a small warm rectangle far down the road. The light is going.

**2:03 — "Somebody like you, somebody like"** — *cut to 7.2*: The same opening
move again, wider and darker, with more of them in it. The rhyme is the point:
one journey, seen twice.

**2:07 — "Everybody like you, ayy"**: They start arriving in numbers, walking
in off the road behind him and settling into the fields on either side. Dozens.
Then hundreds.

**2:10 — "Somebody like you, oh"**: Full night. The camera arcs behind again as
the valley fills. A corridor stays open along the sightline — whatever else is
happening, he is never blocked.

**2:15 — "Somebody like you, ayy"**: The city lets go. Towers, window light,
sodium haze, asphalt, hardpan — all of it dissolves together, and underneath is

**2:19 — "Somebody like you, somebody like"**: He slows, and stops on the road

**2:22 — "Everybody like you"**: The camera settles behind his shoulder.

### ACT 8 — ARIRANG (2:22 – 2:56)

**Music**: Silence (2:22–2:23) → *"아리랑, 아리랑, 아라리요..."*

**SET**: The camera pulls back from the house for the first time. We've been inside or at the doorway for three acts. Now we see the road — and it's not empty anymore. Not just the seven and the golden figure. Dozens of stick figures. Then hundreds. Coming from every direction — down the road, across the paddies, over the stone walls, through the fields. Converging on this small house in the countryside.

These aren't all Korean stick figures. They're every shape a person can be. Tall and thin, short and wide, hunched with age, bouncing with youth. Figures carrying children on their backs — echoing the parent from Act 3. Figures walking arm in arm. Figures alone but heading the same direction. Some carry a bag, a small light, a bundle. They settle into the fields around the house, around the road.

The house is one small warm point at the center of a landscape filling with people. The paddy water reflects everything — figures, sky, mountains — creating a mirror world below. A crowd of strangers who all found the same road.

**2:23 — "아리랑, 아리랑, 아라리요"** *(Arirang, Arirang, Arariyo)*

The golden figure steps out of the house. Stands on the road. And sings. Not performs — sings. The posture softens. Just a person in a field, singing the oldest song they know. The one the grandparent at the table sang. The one that existed before BTS, before K-pop, before stadiums, before electricity.

And the hundreds of figures begin to sway. Together. Not choreographed — each at their own rhythm, their own amplitude. Some barely move. Some rock side to side. A figure carrying a child sways the child too. An elderly figure sways slower. A pair of figures lean into each other. It's the most organic, human movement — a crowd of strangers swaying to a song that every Korean knows and that every human can feel.

The seven are in the crowd. Not on a stage, not elevated, not spotlit. Standing in the paddy water like everyone else. Swaying like everyone else. Indistinguishable except to those who know their silhouettes.

**2:31 — "아리랑 고개로 넘어간다"** *(Crossing over the Arirang pass)*

The camera drifts through the crowd. We see postures that suggest emotion — heads tilted back, hands clasped, two figures leaning into each other, a small figure holding onto a tall figure's leg. Every pair is its own story. Every single figure is its own story. The Arirang pass is everywhere — every person here has crossed it or is crossing it or is waiting for someone on the other side.

**2:39 — "나를 버리고 가시는 님은"** *(The one who abandons me and leaves)*

The lyric that IS Arirang. The golden figure sings it standing on the road in front of the house — the house where the parent waited, where the table was set for one, where the door stayed open. Every person in this field has a 님 — a beloved who left, who they left, who might return. The song holds six hundred years of that ache. The crowd sways with it.

The parent's silhouette is visible in the doorway. Watching the field full of people. Swaying too.

**2:47 — "십리도 못가서 발병난다"** *(Won't get ten li before their feet ache)*

The crowd stills for one beat — the whole field holds its breath — then releases. A collective exhale visualized as warmth: the stick figures glow, the fireflies multiply, the paddy water catches the light. They couldn't stay away. Their feet ached. They all came back — to this field, to this song, to each other.

**THE FRAME**: Hundreds of stick figures in a rice paddy countryside. The small house with its door open, warm light inside, parent in the doorway. The golden figure on the road. The seven scattered in the crowd. Mountains on the horizon. Moon above. A field of people who came from everywhere to stand in the same place and sway to the same song.

Darkness-to-light ratio: 70/30. The light is precious because there isn't much of it.

This frame will be tattooed on people.

### ACT 9 — THE FINALE (2:56 – 3:10)

**Music**: *"I need the whole stadium to jump"* (reprise) → end

**SET**: The same countryside. The hundreds of stick figures are still here. The beat drops and the swaying becomes joy — movement, dancing, celebration. Stick figures dancing in rice paddies, on the road, in the yard of the small house. The golden figure dances among the seven — not as performer and audience, but as people at a gathering that turned into a party. Then: the ascent. The figures begin to lift off the ground — slowly, gently, like fireflies rising from a field at dawn. They become dots of light, rising into the night sky. As they rise, they organize — pulling together into constellation patterns against the black. The Big Dipper. Cassiopeia. The same star field from Act 1. The people become the ancient sky. The countryside empties from the bottom up and the sky fills from the bottom up.

**2:56 — "I need the whole stadium to jump"**: The beat HITS. The same lyric that revealed the stadium in Act 2. But there's no stadium. There's a rice field full of people. And they jump. Hundreds of stick figures, jumping once, together, in a rice paddy. Water splashes. Fireflies scatter upward.

**3:00 — "Put your phone down, let's get all the fun"**: The jumping becomes floating. The figures begin to rise — first the ones at the edges of the field, like embers lifting from a cooling fire. Then waves of them. Detaching from the earth, drifting upward, still glowing warm. The paddy water reflects them going up the same way it reflected them standing still. The parent watches from the doorway. The golden figure watches from the road.

**3:04 — "You at the side, at the back, at the front"**: The camera pulls upward with the rising figures. Aerial view expanding. Below: the countryside — paddies, the house, the road, the mountains on the horizon. Above: hundreds of warm dots organizing into constellations against the dark sky. The same stars that hung over the mountains in Act 1. The people ARE the stars now. Fans becoming the ancient sky.

The seven figures rise too. They join the constellations. The golden figure is the last one on the ground — standing on the road, head tilted up, watching everyone ascend. The parent is in the doorway. Two figures, one road, a sky full of people who were just here.

**3:09 — Final frame**: Maximum altitude. The countryside stretches below — rice paddies, low hills, the winding road, the small house with its warm light, the mountain range on the far horizon. The sky above is dense with new constellations made of warm gold-purple dots, connected by faint lines. Below, on the road, one golden dot. In the doorway, one small warm dot. The house light still on. The door still open.

The sky looks exactly like it did at 0:00 — the same star field, the same moon, the same mountains — except now every star is a person.

**Black.**

## Visual Design Notes

### The Golden Figure
- Color: Warm gold (#D4A843) from the Obangsaek Hwang (황) palette
- 95% scale of the seven (slightly smaller)
- Movement quality: less precise, more human — a fan dancing, not a performer
- **Act 1**: Tiny dot on mountain ridge, same scale as a pine tree. Nearly invisible.
- **Act 2**: One gold dot among 800 purple ARMY bombs in stadium crowd. Brushed by the seventh member at 0:41.
- **Act 3**: The child — carried home, seated at the family table. Warmest frame of the arc.
- **Act 4**: Lifetime compressed. Industrial timelapse (4.1) → relationship vignettes (4.2 first steps, 4.3 persimmon lift, 4.4 sickbed care, 4.5 doorway series at three ages, 4.6 sickbed reversed, 4.7 persimmon hand-down, 4.8 piggyback inversion, 4.9 the PAT). Child becomes adult; caregiver dims a shade each beat.
- **Act 5**: Separates from home across four beats — waves from the road (5.1), plunges into the grey city crowd (5.2), eats alone in a city apartment (5.3). Still unchanged gold. - **Act 7**: Returning. Leading the seven back down the road to the house.
- **Act 8**: Singing Arirang on the road in front of the house. Centered for the first time.
